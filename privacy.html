Privacy

Short version: your files never touch us, because there is nothing of ours for them to touch.

There is no Telepath server

The app talks to Telegram directly, the same way the official client does, using TDLib — Telegram's own client library. Your files, your messages and your Telegram session stay between your Mac and Telegram. We operate no servers, no relay and no analytics, so there is no place where your data could accumulate on our side.

What stays on your Mac

  • Your Telegram session, in the app's own container.
  • An index of your files — names, sizes and dates — in a local database, so Finder can list them without asking Telegram every time.
  • Copies of files you have opened, until the cache limit you set drops them.
  • Your licence key, in the macOS Keychain.

Disconnect and delete data in the app erases all of it. Your messages and files in Telegram are left untouched — deleting an app must never delete an archive.

What leaves your Mac, and to whom

Two things, and nothing else:

  • Telegram receives what you would send it anyway: your sign-in, your file requests, and the files you choose to store. Their privacy policy applies.
  • Polar, our payment provider, receives your purchase details when you buy, and your licence key when the app checks it is still valid. With that check the app sends a salted hash of your Telegram user id — an opaque string. It lets the licence follow your account instead of your computer; it cannot be turned back into your account, and Polar never learns whose Telegram it belongs to.

The app also asks GitHub for the update feed when it checks for a new version. That request contains no identifier of you.

Refunds

Polar acts as the merchant of record and handles refunds. If Telepath is not what you expected, write to support within 14 days of purchase and you will get your money back. The 7-day trial exists so that this rarely has to happen — it is the same app, unrestricted.

Diagnostics

The report the app can save for support contains version numbers, service states and counters. File names and chat titles are deliberately excluded — a product sold on privacy has no business asking you to send a list of your files. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you attach the report yourself.
